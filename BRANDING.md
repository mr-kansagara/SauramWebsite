# SAURAM — Brand Building Roadmap

> How to turn SAURAM (products by MK Enterprise, Bhayavadar, Rajkot, Gujarat) from a product line into a protected, recognizable brand.
> Products: pulses, whole spices, cold-pressed oil, Gir cow ghee.
>
> Companion docs: `DESIGN.md` (visual identity / design tokens), `PAGES.md` (page specs).

## TL;DR — Where to start

Since the name (SAURAM), logo direction, and website already exist, the real first step is:

1. **Legally secure the name** — trademark search + filing (time-sensitive: cheap now, expensive to fix later if someone else registers it first).
2. **Write down the brand positioning** — a ~2-page brand foundation document.

Everything else (packaging, marketing, social media) builds on those two things.

## Step 1 — Protect the name (urgent, time-sensitive)

### Trademark

- [x] **Done (2026-08-13) — public search on [IP India TM Public Search](https://ipindiaservices.gov.in/tmrpublicsearch) confirmed "SAURAM" is available** in the relevant classes:
  - **Class 29** — ghee, edible oils, processed pulses
  - **Class 30** — spices
- [ ] **File the trademark application — the current top-priority action.** Availability isn't protection; the name is only secured once an application is on file (first-to-file matters in India):
  - Government fee: **₹4,500 per class** for individuals / small enterprises (with MSME/Udyam registration), **₹9,000** otherwise.
  - A local trademark agent in Rajkot typically charges ~₹1,500–3,000 on top, or self-file at [ipindia.gov.in](https://www.ipindia.gov.in).
  - You can use the **™** symbol immediately after filing; **®** only after registration is granted.
- [ ] Get **MSME/Udyam registration** for MK Enterprise first if not done — it halves the TM government fee.

> Note: FSSAI registration does **not** protect the brand name. Trademark and FSSAI are separate laws and can be pursued in parallel.

## Step 2 — Brand foundation document (~2 pages)

The strategic core of the brand. Sits *above* `DESIGN.md` and should drive every future design and copy decision. Answer these on paper:

### Target customer

"Everyone who eats food" is not an answer. Candidate segments:

- Urban Gujarati families who miss village-quality ghee.
- Health-conscious buyers who pay a premium for cold-pressed (not refined) oil.
- NRI / gifting market for traditional Gir cow ghee.

Pick 1–2 primary segments and describe them concretely (age, city, where they shop, what frustrates them about current options).

### Positioning

Why SAURAM instead of Patanjali, Tata Sampann, or loose kirana stock?

Real differentiators already in hand:

- **Gir cow ghee** (breed-specific, premium)
- **Cold-pressed oil** (process-specific, health angle)
- **Direct from Bhayavadar/Rajkot** — authentic farm origin + traditional process story that large competitors can't claim authentically.

### Story, mission, values, voice

- **Brand story** — the founders, the farm, why SAURAM exists.
- **Mission & values** — 3–5 values, each one sentence.
- **Tone of voice** — e.g. warm, honest, rooted in Gujarat; not corporate.

### Checklist framework — the 4 C's

| C | Question |
|---|----------|
| **Clarity** | Is it obvious what SAURAM stands for? |
| **Consistency** | Same visuals, messaging, tone on every platform? |
| **Credibility** | Are we delivering on every promise (purity, origin)? |
| **Competitiveness** | Is the positioning distinct in the market? |

## Step 3 — Packaging (where brand meets law)

For a food brand, packaging **is** the brand — it's what customers see on the shelf. In India, every label must satisfy **two separate frameworks at once** (passing one does not mean passing the other):

### FSSAI Labelling & Display Regulations 2020

Every pre-packaged food label must carry (in English or Hindi):

- FSSAI logo + 14-digit license number
- Common/generic name of the food (not just the brand name)
- Full ingredients list
- Nutrition information panel
- Veg symbol (green dot)
- Batch/lot number (traceable to production records)
- Allergen declarations where applicable

### Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules 2011

- Net quantity in metric units — g/kg for solids, ml/l for liquids
- MRP (inclusive of all taxes)
- Manufacturer/packer name and full address
- Month and year of manufacture
- Consumer care contact details

- [ ] **Ghee:** look into **AGMARK** certification — a strong trust signal for ghee buyers in India.
